# Summary
A tag for every reactor scram since 1999 saying what started it (the system, and whether testing,
maintenance, an error or weather was involved) and a cost for each one (the output it lost until
the unit came back). The result is a `/scrams` page that answers which triggers trip reactors
most often and which ones cost the most energy, and a `trigger=` filter in the event explorer.

# Problem
The trends page counts scrams (1,822 unit scrams on 1,802 Event Notifications through 2026-11-19)
and splits them into automatic and manual, but says nothing about why they happened. The ledger
gives every day a scram costs to one fine cause, `scram`, in the Forced family, so "which kind of
scram costs the most output" has no answer anywhere in the app. The NRC's scram dataset carries
no cause at all, and its performance indicators are rates (Decision 33).

A scratch keyword pass on 2026-11-20 (PRD-0007.log.md) showed the answer is worth having:

- Feedwater and condensate start about a quarter of all scrams and improved slowest.
- Scrams during testing and maintenance fell about 80% from 1999–2005 to 2020–2025, and
  grid-driven ones about 90%.
- Turbine controls didn't improve at all.
- The four BWR/6 units scram at about twice the fleet's rate per unit-year.
- Scrams from low power cluster at the restarts after spring and fall refueling.

Those numbers came from untested regexes, so none of them can go on a page or into a finding yet.

# Goals
1. Every scram EN tagged with a system and a circumstance, with a measured accuracy.
2. Every scram's episode: how long the unit stayed below its pre-scram power, and the energy lost.
3. Scram rates and lost output by system over the years, with the circumstance beside them.
4. A unit table, so the units that scram most stand out with their rate and leading trigger.
5. The trigger reachable from the explorer and on each scram's event page.

# Technical Approach
- **The tags.** `downpower_ingest::triggers` reads the title and the trip sentences of each scram
  EN (any unit with a SCRAM Code other than `N`, unretracted). Ordered phrase rules in
  `catalog/triggers.toml` set each of two dimensions, the first match winning, as
  `catalog/tags.toml` and `catalog/ffd.toml` work. Rule order puts a root trigger ahead of the
  signal it causes: "a turbine trip due to a loss of offsite power" is offsite power, and "a low
  steam generator level after a feed pump trip" is feedwater.
  - **System**, seven values plus `not_named`:
    1. Feedwater and condensate.
    2. Turbine and generator, including the turbine controls.
    3. Plant electrical: transformers, buses, breakers, inverters.
    4. Offsite power and the grid.
    5. Cooling water: condenser vacuum, circulating water, the intake.
    6. Reactor and coolant system: control rods, coolant and recirculation pumps, the
       pressurizer, main steam isolation.
    7. Instruments and protection: spurious signals, failed transmitters and cards.
  - **Circumstance**: testing, maintenance, an operator or technician error, weather, or `none`.
  - A reviewed `assign` list in the same file overrides either tag for a named event, each entry
    with a reason. `cargo xtask triggers` writes `catalog/triggers.tsv` (number, date, docket,
    kind, system, circumstance, the rule that matched), and its diff is reviewed like the reasons
    snapshot's.
- **Accuracy.** T-001 labels 150 scram ENs by hand before any rule exists. The rules are tuned on
  the archive's 1,802 texts, the sample scores them, and the agreement is logged with the
  confusions.
- **Episodes.** For each EN scram, the unit's ledger rows from the day of the event or the next,
  whichever first shows the drop (`scrams::DROP`, the scram check's rule). The episode ends:
  - the day before the unit is back within 10 points of its pre-scram power;
  - at the first day attributed to Refueling, since a scram just before an outage would
    otherwise book the outage;
  - at the unit's next EN scram, which starts its own episode;
  - or at the end of service or of the data, flagged open.

  Its energy is (pre-scram power − power)% × net capacity × 24 h over those rows, whatever cause
  each row is attributed to. A scram the reports don't show, or one from under 10% power, has no
  episode and still counts in the rates.
- **The series.** Computed in the rebuild beside the trends: each scram with its tags and episode,
  and operating unit-days by year and by unit. It is served at `/api/scrams`, and the page
  aggregates it, as `/ffd` does.
- **The page.** `/scrams` shares the trends' `ViewState` range, so a link carries across. It
  shows:
  - scrams per 100 operating unit-years by system, stacked by year;
  - lost output by system as a share of net capacity energy, by year;
  - scrams per 100 unit-years with each circumstance;
  - a unit table: scrams, per unit-year, median episode length, and leading system, each row
    linking to `/events?unit=…&scram=1`.

  The systems take a new categorical palette, since the family colors mean families.

# Assumptions
- PRD-0003's parse keeps each scram EN's title, text and unit table, and the scram check's 99.7%
  agreement means nearly every EN scram has ledger rows to measure an episode on.
- Keywords in the trip sentences name the system often enough for a 90% agreement: a "not named"
  label agrees with a "not named" tag, so an EN that says only "under investigation" is easy.
- The categories line up with the initiating-event groups the NRC's risk programs use (loss of
  feedwater, loss of offsite power, loss of condenser heat sink), so a later cross-check against
  them stays possible.

# Constraints
- No new source: root causes from Licensee Event Reports are a separate PRD.
- One axis per chart, at most eight categorical colors (dataviz skill), so seven systems plus a
  neutral `not_named`.
- Stable toolchain 1.99, no chart or component library.

# Non-Goals (MVP)
- Root causes from Licensee Event Reports.
- A view by power at the scram (the restart clustering) or by reactor design; the unit table
  carries the design question for now.
- Findings (Decision 25). They come after this, from the tested series.
- Re-attributing the ledger: episodes measure a scram's cost beside the attribution and change no
  row's cause.
