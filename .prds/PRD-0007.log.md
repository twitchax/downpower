# PRD-0007 log

## Draft -- 2026-11-20

The scratch analysis behind the PRD. It ran a throwaway binary against `downpower-ingest`
(`EventSet::build` over the local raw store, no fetch) and keyword regexes in Python. None of it is
committed, and none of the numbers below are tested: T-001 and T-002 replace them.

**What was counted.** Every unretracted Event Notification whose unit table gives a unit a SCRAM
Code other than `N`: 1,802 events and 1,822 unit scrams, 1999-01-01 to 2026-11-19. 40% have a
manual code. Each event got one trigger from the first of 17 ordered keyword families that matched
its title and the first two trip sentences of its text (the text before the first update).

**Accuracy.** Spot checks of six events per family found the families right in aggregate, with
misfires a real tagger has to handle:

- `loop` matched "loop 1" of a PWR's coolant loops as a loss of offsite power;
- `seismic` matched a loss of seismic monitoring;
- `fire` matched texts where the fire was an aside;
- `transformer` matched the reserve transformer the buses transferred to after the trip;
- `testing` matched testing that was in progress but didn't cause the trip.

722 of the 1,802 (40%) say the cause is under investigation or unknown when filed.

The two steepest declines hold on the whole text, not just the trip sentences: mentions of
testing, surveillance or maintenance fall from 25.9 events a year to 6.3, and of offsite power,
the switchyard or a transmission line from 11.3 to 1.7.

**Reactor type**, from the event's RX Type: BWRs 725 events (40%), PWRs 1,077. The four BWR/6
units: Grand Gulf 42, River Bend 37, Clinton 33, Perry 25, 137 in all, about 1.2 per unit-year
against 0.65 for the fleet (1,802 over the ledger's 1,017,404 unit-days). The most scrams by unit
since 2016: Grand Gulf 1 with 20, Watts Bar 2 with 17.

**Month of the year.** Scrams from under 30% power: April 45, May 52, October 39, November 41,
against 9 to 28 in each other month, so they follow the refueling seasons. May has the most scrams
(199, against 123 to 163 elsewhere). Scrams from 90% or more peak in August (131).

**2026.** 19 events from 01-01 to 11-19, against 36, 47 and 42 over the same dates in 2025, 2024
and 2023. A Poisson at the three-year mean of 41.7 gives 19 or fewer a probability of 0.00007.
Fewer scrams in every family: feedwater 11 to 3, turbine controls 5 to 0, automatic 19 to 9,
manual 17 to 10.

**Titles.** A title that names no trigger (`AUTOMATIC REACTOR TRIP`, `... ACTUATION ...`) is 76
of 661 events in 1999–2005 (11%) and 138 of 226 since 2021 (61%), so a tagger reading titles alone
would lose most of the recent years. T-004 records it as a break.

## T-001 -- 2026-11-23

**The draw.** The 1,802 unretracted scram ENs in the home-box archive (through 2026-11-19), sorted
by number, and `random.Random(20261123).sample(numbers, 150)` in Python. By decade: 8 from 1999,
69 from 2000–2009, 55 from 2010–2019, 18 from 2020–2026. The events, as `Event` JSON, are in
`crates/downpower-ingest/tests/fixtures/scrams/sample.jsonl` and their labels in `labeled.tsv`.

**The labels.** Labeled in three batches of 50 from the full text, updates included, following
`labeling-guide.md` in the same directory, before any rule existed and without the scratch regexes
of the draft. Each label carries a `sure` flag, and 39 of 150 came back `no`. A review of all 39,
and of 16 of the sure ones, changed three:

- EN 40301: `feedwater` to `reactor_coolant`. The text names high reactor coolant conductivity, and
  the condensate demineralizer is only the licensee's speculation.
- EN 41593: `human_error` to `none`. The lineup that drained one steam generator into another is
  never called an error.
- EN 56075: `electrical` to `feedwater`. The trip is a main feedwater pump's, and an electrical
  transient is only suspected.

**What the labels show.** Systems: turbine and generator 40, feedwater 33, reactor and coolant
system 25, electrical 17, cooling water 13, instruments 9, offsite power 9, not named 4.
Circumstances: none 127, testing 17, maintenance 3, weather 2, human error 1.

Only 4 name no system, against the 40% of ENs that leave the root cause under investigation: the
guide gives a text naming only the protective signal the signal's own system, so "a turbine trip,
cause under investigation" is turbine and generator. Decision 41 is revised to say so.

**A later ruling on hedged causes** (2026-11-23, after T-002). The labelers counted a cause given
with a hedge ("believed", "apparently", "most probably") as named in nearly every case, and the
review above had gone the other way on two. For consistency the guide now says a hedged cause
counts, and EN 40301 and EN 56075 went back to the labelers' `feedwater` and `electrical`. The
sample's agreement stayed 135 of 150: one more label agrees and one fewer.

## T-002 -- 2026-11-23

**How the rules read an event** (`catalog/triggers.toml`). The title, then the text's sentences,
updates included, lowercased, with the response boilerplate blanked: rods in, auxiliary
feedwater, decay heat, levels being maintained, the buses moving to their backup transformer,
diesels, relief valves lifting, isolations, the reporting paragraph and the quoted rule text. The
system is the first root-cause rule in the earliest sentence any root or signal rule matches. A
lead that names only a signal ("a turbine trip") takes the first root named in the next two
sentences or in any sentence with a word of cause, and after that only a cause sentence naming a
root further up the rule order changes it. The circumstance is the first circumstance rule in the
sentences about the trip.

**Scores.** Every number is agreement with the hand labels, systems then circumstances.

| Stage | Sample (150) | Validation (50) |
|---|---|---|
| First complete rule set | 105, 136 | |
| Tuned on the first 100 only; the last 50 (2012–2026) held out | 89/100 and 37/50 held out | |
| Tuned on all 150 | 135, 145 | |
| A fresh 50, drawn after tuning (seed 20261124) and labeled the same way | | **39, 43** |
| After general fixes from those misses | 135, 145 | 43, 44 |

The fresh draw is the honest measure: **the rules agree on the system for about 4 scrams in 5 they
weren't tuned on (39 of 50), and on the circumstance for 43 of 50.** Restricted to the labels the
labelers were sure of, the sample agreed on 98 of 111. `uat_scram_triggers_sample` holds the
sample at 90% (the PRD's bar) and the validation fixture at 85%, as regression guards.

Most of the remaining misses are judgment calls the labelers flagged too: an instrument fault
during testing read as the signal it tripped (overtemperature delta temperature), a recirculation
pump's electrical fault as the pump, a station transformer behind a loss of offsite power named in
the title. Three were bugs, fixed: `generator` matched "steam generator", `led to` matched "failed
to", and "testing" in the quoted 10 CFR 50.72 text tagged circumstances.

## T-004 -- 2026-11-23

`docs/BREAKS.md` now covers both series: its fitness-for-duty entries moved under their own
heading, and a scram section joined them. Its four entries:

- the 2001 rewrite of 10 CFR 50.72 (65 FR 63769, effective 2001-01-23), which moved scrams from
  (b)(2)(ii) to (b)(2)(iv)(B) and (b)(3)(iv)(A): the first new code is EN 37691 on 2001-01-25 and
  the last old one EN 37994 on 2001-05-16, with 100 scram events in 2000 and 101 in 2001;
- titles that stopped naming the trigger (11% of 1999–2005 scram events, 61% since 2021);
- the NRC's revised texts (80 scram events, 2020–2026);
- the `A` and `M` SCRAM Codes (23 and 5 units, against 1,794 coded `A/R` or `M/R`).

The citation was checked against the Federal Register's own page and the 2000 edition of 10 CFR
on govinfo, not nrc.gov. That check corrected the first draft of the entry: the rule also stopped
invalid scrams from a shut-down reactor being phoned in, which the data shows a year late (unit
scrams of a reactor that wasn't critical: 10, 8 and 9 in 1999–2001, then 2 to 5 a year).

## T-006 -- 2026-11-23

**The palette** (`style/_systems.scss`). The dataviz reference hues again, since the design
language's chart tokens stop at three. Every assignment of seven of the eight hues to the stack
order was scored with the dataviz validator against both chart surfaces; 2,028 passed, and the one
kept ties for the best worst-adjacent pair (colorblind ΔE 16.3 light, 9.9 dark; normal vision 19.6
and 19.3). It gives offsite power the grid family's violet and not named Unattributed's gray, so
the two hues that carry over between pages keep their meaning.

**Looked at** on the full archive at 1,360 and 390 px, on a release build served locally with no
fetching. Two problems, both fixed:

- At 390 px the page was 591 px wide: the unit table widened the page's grid instead of scrolling
  in place, and the eight-system legend didn't wrap (`4480847`).
- The 16 largest episodes carry 22% of all episode energy, so their tags were read by hand. Twelve
  of the 14 read were right. D.C. Cook 1's 2008 turbine fire (EN 44507, 10.8 TWh) was tagged
  cooling water, because "main condenser vacuum was broken to stop the turbine due to high
  vibration" named a cause further upstream, and Indian Point 2's 2000 steam generator tube
  rupture (EN 36695) named no system. Both were fixed by general rules, not overrides
  (`5f4ad00`): turbine fires and vibration, tube ruptures and primary-to-secondary leaks, and
  breaking vacuum as a response. Neither fixture moved (135 and 145 of 150; 43 and 44 of 50).

**The test binary.** Adding the page pushed the `serve` test binary's debug strings past 4 GiB,
beyond 32-bit DWARF offsets, and it stopped linking under mold. Dependencies now build without
debug info in dev and test builds; the workspace's own crates keep it (`c0518fe`).
