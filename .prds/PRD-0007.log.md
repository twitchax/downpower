# PRD-0007 log

## Draft -- 2026-11-20

The scratch analysis behind the PRD. It ran a throwaway binary against `downpower-ingest`
(`EventSet::build` over the local raw store, no fetch) and keyword regexes in Python. None of it is
committed, and none of the numbers below are tested: T-001 and T-002 replace them.

**What was counted.** Every unretracted Event Notification whose unit table gives a unit a SCRAM
Code other than `N`: 1,802 events and 1,822 unit scrams, 1999-01-01 to 2026-11-19. 40% have a
manual code. Each event got one trigger from the first of 17 ordered keyword families that matched
its title and the first two trip sentences of its text (the text before the first update).

**Accuracy.** Spot checks of six events per family found the families right in aggregate, with
misfires a real tagger has to handle:

- `loop` matched "loop 1" of a PWR's coolant loops as a loss of offsite power;
- `seismic` matched a loss of seismic monitoring;
- `fire` matched texts where the fire was an aside;
- `transformer` matched the reserve transformer the buses transferred to after the trip;
- `testing` matched testing that was in progress but didn't cause the trip.

722 of the 1,802 (40%) say the cause is under investigation or unknown when filed.

The two steepest declines hold on the whole text, not just the trip sentences: mentions of
testing, surveillance or maintenance fall from 25.9 events a year to 6.3, and of offsite power,
the switchyard or a transmission line from 11.3 to 1.7.

**Reactor type**, from the event's RX Type: BWRs 725 events (40%), PWRs 1,077. The four BWR/6
units: Grand Gulf 42, River Bend 37, Clinton 33, Perry 25, 137 in all, about 1.2 per unit-year
against 0.65 for the fleet (1,802 over the ledger's 1,017,404 unit-days). The most scrams by unit
since 2016: Grand Gulf 1 with 20, Watts Bar 2 with 17.

**Month of the year.** Scrams from under 30% power: April 45, May 52, October 39, November 41,
against 9 to 28 in each other month, so they follow the refueling seasons. May has the most scrams
(199, against 123 to 163 elsewhere). Scrams from 90% or more peak in August (131).

**2026.** 19 events from 01-01 to 11-19, against 36, 47 and 42 over the same dates in 2025, 2024
and 2023. A Poisson at the three-year mean of 41.7 gives 19 or fewer a probability of 0.00007.
Fewer scrams in every family: feedwater 11 to 3, turbine controls 5 to 0, automatic 19 to 9,
manual 17 to 10.

**Titles.** A title that names no trigger (`AUTOMATIC REACTOR TRIP`, `... ACTUATION ...`) is 76
of 661 events in 1999–2005 (11%) and 138 of 226 since 2021 (61%), so a tagger reading titles alone
would lose most of the recent years. T-004 records it as a break.

## T-001 -- 2026-11-23

**The draw.** The 1,802 unretracted scram ENs in the home-box archive (through 2026-11-19), sorted
by number, and `random.Random(20261123).sample(numbers, 150)` in Python. By decade: 8 from 1999,
69 from 2000–2009, 55 from 2010–2019, 18 from 2020–2026. The events, as `Event` JSON, are in
`crates/downpower-ingest/tests/fixtures/scrams/sample.jsonl` and their labels in `labeled.tsv`.

**The labels.** Labeled in three batches of 50 from the full text, updates included, following
`labeling-guide.md` in the same directory, before any rule existed and without the scratch regexes
of the draft. Each label carries a `sure` flag, and 39 of 150 came back `no`. A review of all 39,
and of 16 of the sure ones, changed three:

- EN 40301: `feedwater` to `reactor_coolant`. The text names high reactor coolant conductivity, and
  the condensate demineralizer is only the licensee's speculation.
- EN 41593: `human_error` to `none`. The lineup that drained one steam generator into another is
  never called an error.
- EN 56075: `electrical` to `feedwater`. The trip is a main feedwater pump's, and an electrical
  transient is only suspected.

**What the labels show.** Systems: turbine and generator 40, feedwater 33, reactor and coolant
system 25, electrical 17, cooling water 13, instruments 9, offsite power 9, not named 4.
Circumstances: none 127, testing 17, maintenance 3, weather 2, human error 1.

Only 4 name no system, against the 40% of ENs that leave the root cause under investigation: the
guide gives a text naming only the protective signal the signal's own system, so "a turbine trip,
cause under investigation" is turbine and generator. Decision 41 is revised to say so.
