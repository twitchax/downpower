**Drug-related fitness-for-duty reports ran between 2.9 and 11.7 per 100 operating unit-years from 1999 to 2025.** They averaged 7.9 through 2011, when no plant sat in a state with legal adult use, and 5.6 since, as that share of the fleet's unit-years climbed to 43.8% (2024).

That count is a *floor*, and it sinks as reports stop naming the substance. From 1999 to 2011, 18% of the reports from operating reactors named none. From 2021 to 2025, 53% did, and so far in 2026, 79% (e.g., someone "failed a test specified by the fitness-for-duty testing program"). An event that names no drug can't count as drug-related. Every FFD event together, which the naming doesn't touch, rose from 24 per 100 unit-years in 1999 to 42 in 2025, and the reports don't say how much of that rise involves drugs.

These are only the events 10 CFR 26.719 makes reportable within 24 hours (e.g., drugs or alcohol in a protected area, or a licensed operator's positive test). A single state sees one to three drug-related events a year, far too few to estimate an effect of its laws.[^1]

[^1]: Part 26's 2008 rewrite changed what is reported, so counts on either side of it don't compare directly. The legalization dates are hand-curated from Wikipedia's timeline and checked against NCSL and the statutes. Each event counts in the plant's state, wherever the worker lives.
