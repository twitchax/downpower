**In 1999–2001 the fleet lost 12.5% of its net capacity to reported power reductions, and in 2016–2025 it lost 8.7%.** That is almost a third less, measured as a share of net capacity, so the fleet's shrinking from about 103 operating units to about 94 over the same years doesn't count toward it.

The days the reports put down to refueling and maintenance account for more than the whole drop. Refueling fell from 7.9% of capacity to 5.9%, as the median refueling outage shortened from 36 days in 1999 to between 29 and 34 since 2016. Maintenance (the outages and derates a report calls planned) fell from 2.7% to 0.7%. Together that is 4.0 points, against a total drop of 3.8.

Forced outages moved the *other* way, from 0.7% to 1.2%, and they carry the chart's lumps (2.1% to 3.0% a year from 2011 to 2013).[^1]

[^1]: A day's loss is its output below full power on the NRC's daily status report, at the unit's net capacity, booked to the cause family its reason names (the *attributed* layer). The period averages weight each year by its capacity. Twenty-seven years of reasons were written by different people, so the line between a forced outage and a maintenance outage is the report's own, and is *directional rather than precise*.
